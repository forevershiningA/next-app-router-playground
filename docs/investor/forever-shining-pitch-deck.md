# Forever Shining

# The problem

## A high-consideration purchase still runs on fragmented, offline workflows.

- Families must make emotionally difficult, highly personal choices with limited visual certainty.
- Makers and funeral providers lose time to back-and-forth on dimensions, inscriptions, imagery, materials and cemetery requirements.
- Quotes are often manual and design proofs hard to share, revise and retain.
- Local operators have deep craft knowledge, but rarely have modern product configuration, digital acquisition or conversion tooling.

**Result:** lower confidence, slower approval cycles and expensive operational friction.

# The product

## A proven online sales workflow today, with a next-generation 3D studio in development.

1. **Established company:** Forever Shining has operated in Australia for **20 years**; its existing online configurator has supported real customer orders for approximately seven years.
2. **Next platform:** a modern, real-time 3D studio will let families configure products, materials, dimensions, inscriptions, imagery and additions with greater visual certainty.
3. **Commercial workflow:** the platform roadmap connects design, quote, proof approval and order handoff while preserving the operating knowledge built in the live business.

The investment case is not a speculative configurator: it is the modernisation and scaling of an operating memorial-commerce workflow with demonstrated demand.

# Why now

## Consumer expectations have moved online; memorial commerce has not caught up.

- Families expect transparent pricing, self-service research and a visual proof before committing.
- Search behaviour already spans material, motif, relationship and product-intent queries — ideal for inspiration-led acquisition.
- The Australian death-care market is large and fragmented. An industry report estimates FY24 Australian death-care revenue at **A$2.8bn**; fragmentation creates a practical distribution wedge for white-label software and operator partnerships.[^market]
- The same configuration problem repeats across geographies, languages and local cemetery rules.

[^market]: Kroll / InvoCare scheme booklet, 2023, page 61: <https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20230925/pdf/05v87t664yx9mg.pdf>. Market figure is third-party industry context, not company revenue.

# The wedge

## Build from an operating D2C base. Extend into a scalable platform.

| Established D2C business | Next — B2B2C platform |
|---|---|
| SEO-led discovery and inspirational design gallery | White-label studio for stonemasons, funeral homes and memorial parks |
| Existing configurator and order operations | Dealer catalogue, margin rules, quoting, lead routing and proof approval |
| In-house fulfilment for easy-to-ship plaques and small headstones | Local production and installation network for complex monuments |

**Business model:** a hybrid fulfilment engine — own product margin where shipping and production are predictable; partner production/installation margin or commission where the product is large, complex or location-dependent; software fees or transaction fees only where a partner uses the platform independently.

## Fulfilment follows product complexity, not a one-size-fits-all supply chain.

- **Easy-shipping plaques:** produced and fulfilled in-house for speed, quality control and margin.
- **Small headstones:** fulfilled in-house where logistics and installation are manageable.
- **Full monuments:** sourced through factories and local partners where that lowers transport cost and gives families access to qualified installers.
- **Platform control remains central:** Forever Shining owns the digital design, pricing logic, proofing, customer journey and partner-routing rules.

# The advantage

## Operating knowledge plus software that understands the physical product.

- **20 years** of Australian market, customer, product, pricing and fulfilment experience in a difficult, high-consideration category — including approximately seven years of live online configuration.
- Established product, pricing and production knowledge informs the catalogue and workflow of the new platform.
- The next-generation build adds real-time 3D rendering, a reusable catalogue/pricing engine, saved/shareable designs and a more scalable quote-to-order flow.
- The platform can be adapted to a partner catalogue rather than rebuilt for each supplier.

**Defensibility is earned through catalogue depth, manufacturing integrations, conversion data and partner distribution.** It should not be represented as a completed moat today.

# Go-to-market

## Win trust locally, then scale through distribution.

1. **Australia D2C:** intent SEO, design-gallery content, paid search around high-intent categories, assisted online design and in-house fulfilment for simple products.
2. **Regional partner network:** pair complex monument orders with suitable factories and installers to reduce freight and installation friction.
3. **Operator pilots:** give selected stonemasons/funeral providers a branded studio for their catalogue, leads and proof approvals.
4. **Channel expansion:** industry associations, funeral-home groups, cemetery/memorial-park relationships and manufacturers.
5. **Internationalisation:** local catalogues, measurement conventions, currency/tax and cemetery requirements — only after the unit-economics and partner playbook work in Australia.

**Distribution principle:** the platform augments local experts and craft; it does not attempt to replace them.

# Market strategy

## A narrow beachhead can support a much larger software outcome.

**Beachhead:** Australian custom plaques and headstones, where the team has 20 years of operating experience and already runs a live, revenue-generating configurator and order workflow.

**Expansion:** broader memorial products → independent operators → multi-site funeral and memorial-park networks → English-speaking markets.

**Sizing discipline:** do not present a top-down TAM as revenue. Before investor meetings, build a bottom-up model from: addressable suppliers × catalogue/transaction volume × achievable software or take-rate revenue. Attach named market sources and state the geography and year for every figure.
